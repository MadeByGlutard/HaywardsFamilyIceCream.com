# Hayward’s in Milford: history research

Redone November 23, 2026, replacing the November 16 version. One researcher started from scratch without seeing the old notes; the claims used on `/history` were then checked in the sources themselves. Keep Milford (383 Elm Street) apart from Nashua and Merrimack.

## What changed from the first version

- **1939, not undated.** The first version left the opening year out. A 1962 trade-journal obituary says Stanley Hayward built the stand in 1939, Milford History Online says it opened that summer, and a town building permit (1939–40) and the April 1942 tax list fit. The 1940 date belongs to the Nashua stand (June 15, 1940). The Cabinet’s “1957, the ice cream stand” is contradicted by the 1942 and 1952 tax lists.
- **The building is from 2001**, not the original (town assessor; Hayward’s own 2009 site: “Open in 2001 it sits on the Property of what once was Hayward Farms”). No source shows a Milford stand between the late 1960s or 70s and 2001. **Never write “continuously since 1939”.**
- **The ice cream was made elsewhere** from the late 1970s until January 10, 2008 (Hayward’s 2008 site).
- Frank Robinson, unnamed on the page, was the 2008 batchmaster, an owner by 2012 and the 2022 spokesman. Living people stay off the page unless Hayward’s asks.

## Postcards

Both cards are in the Boston Public Library’s Tichnor Brothers Collection, marked “No known copyright restrictions” and “No known restrictions on use” (checked November 23, 2026). The files in `public/images/postcard-*.jpg` are the library’s scans with the scan edge cropped off; nothing is retouched. The library dates both about 1930–1945, but that is an estimate and the cards carry no date, so the page says “undated”. The card numbers suggest the green-roof card is older.

## Leads, not used

Founding of the family dairy in 1905 (1962 obituary); locker plant (about 1940); smokehouse 1943; snack bar 1951; 65-seat restaurant (1954 or 1956); sale to Idlenot Dairy after a 1966 fire (Cabinet 2016 only, contradicted in part); “first major highway ice cream stand in New Hampshire” (1962 obituary only); “20 franchises” and the “Big Chief” statue (Milford History Online only; the statue is a Native American caricature); “150 cows” (company only; the 1952 tax list shows 84). The curved roof is today’s building: both postcards show a hipped roof.
