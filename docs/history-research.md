# Hayward’s Family Ice Cream — Milford history research

Research date: November 16, 2026. Scope: 383 Elm Street, Milford, NH; haywardsfamilyicecream.com. This is not a history page for the present Nashua/Merrimack business.

## Recommended website copy

### An Elm Street tradition

Hayward’s story begins with local dairy farming. Before the ice cream windows, Charles Hayward worked for Whiting Dairy and bought a farm on Elm Street. His three sons helped carry the family business forward.[1][2]

The roadside stand became part of that story. An early Hayward Farms postcard identifies the Milford building as a place where ice cream was made for its network of stands. It also advertises a snack bar, a reminder that the business has taken different forms over the years.[3]

### A place in Milford’s memories

There is a small piece of that history in Milford High School’s 1948 yearbook: a Hayward Farms advertisement for dairy products, ice cream and frozen foods. It is a tangible link between the business and generations of people who grew up here.[4]

The tradition continued into another chapter in 2022, when Hayward’s Family Ice Cream joined the New Hampshire Ice Cream Trail. Interviewed that summer, Frank Robinson described the Milford shop’s ice cream as homemade and prepared in individual batches.[5]

Today, you will find Hayward’s Family Ice Cream at 383 Elm Street. The menu includes ice cream, frozen yogurt, sherbet, sorbet, vegan and no-sugar-added choices. Come back for a favorite, or find a new one.[6]

Optional small factual note: “Listed on New Hampshire’s 2026 Ice Cream Trail.” This is verified, but the 2026 passport season has ended; do not present it as a current promotion.[7]

## Dates and claims requiring owner/archive confirmation

- Opening year: Bowling explicitly dates Milford’s opening to summer 1939 and says Nashua followed the next summer. New England Historical Society instead says Milford opened in 1940. A 2016 Cabinet recap of a dairy-history talk says a stand was added in 1957. These cannot all describe the same event. Do not put an unqualified “Since 1940” or “Since 1939” in the masthead yet.
- Bowling reports 18 opening flavors and 11,000 gallons sold in the first summer. His narrative also dates a snack bar to 1951 and a 65-seat restaurant to 1956. Retain as research leads pending original newspaper or family records.
- Do not assert that the present building is the original 1939 structure. No construction/continuity evidence found.
- Older accounts connect Chris Ordway and brother-in-law Frank Robinson to a 1996 purchase. The clearest detailed source found is a 2012 personal travel blog, while a 2022 newspaper article describes a shared family business. Confirm the precise Milford ownership timeline and current owners before publishing names, dates, “third generation,” or organizational relationships as present facts.
- Do not import Nashua’s June 15, 1940 opening date, its gas-station history, its building relocation, or Merrimack’s 2019 opening into Milford’s timeline.
- Facebook retrieval returned a temporary-block page, so no new Facebook history or current ownership evidence was obtained in this research pass.
- Historic images: the historian’s postcard is credited to Milford Historical Society; CardCow offers a commercial scan. No clear reusable license was verified. Link these sources; use the business’s existing photos in the prototype until an archive/family image is cleared.

## Suggested history-page structure

- “Our story” page title and short Elm Street lead.
- Real current storefront photo, captioned as a current photograph.
- Three short editorial sections using the copy above.
- A compact archival note linking the 1948 yearbook and local historian; keep source notes available without overwhelming the visitor.
- End with menu and directions links.
- Avoid a falsely precise founding-date badge or an invented multigeneration timeline. Add the verified opening record and a licensed historic image once obtained.
